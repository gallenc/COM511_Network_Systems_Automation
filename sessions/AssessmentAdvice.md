# Assessment Advice

You must try to hit all the categories in the marking criteria and all the customer requirements mentioned in the [Assessment Brief](./AssessmentBrief.md).
I want to give you marks, but I can't if you don't try to meet each criteria

### 1. Structure, cohesion & presentation of report    20% 

Remember who the audience is. 

The customer is technically literate but he is more interested in the business value of the solution than the technical details. 
Your main text needs to lead him towards the conclusion that you understand his problem, and could create a credible solution. 
Anything that is too complex or obscure the message should go in appendices, tables or footnotes.

This document is essentially a sales proposal for the customer which demonstrates your competence and expertise. 
You are showing him that you have listened to him and you understand his problem - possibly better than he does (thats why he would be wise to give you the job). 

You are also proposing possible solutions which will meet his need. 
You need to give him an overview and the benefit of your solution - but put the technical details in the appendix.

Note that for each requirement there may be several possible solutions. 
We have covered one possible solution for each requirement in class, but you should evidence some research to see if there are alternatives which could be considered.

The word count is deliberately quite tight to force you to think about what you really want to say.
So you want to avoid unnecessary detail, waffle and repetition in the main text. 

We do want details to prove understanding of the technology but the main text should be giving the big picture with reference to details in the appendix.

Any words in the executive overview, in footnotes or tables or the appendix do not count towards the word count. Use the main text wisely to show that you understand the technology but avoid cluttering the main narrative. 

Formatting:
* Have a header (title) page, executive overview, glossary, table of contents, page numbers in footers and a version control page (see submission below)
* Use 1.5 line spacing with justified text in the paragraphs.
* Use page breaks before each major section. (i.e. don't use line feeds to align headings to the top of pages).
* Use diagrams to explain your proposal.
* Number your sections and your requirements using legal numbering ( Section 1, 1.1, 1.2 etc.) and refer to the numbered requirements in your solution

### 2. Requirements analysis (What does the customer need)   20%

You have been given a list of high level requirements in the [Assessment Brief](./AssessmentBrief.md). 
These are just the first level of the customer's understanding. 

You need to ensure you demonstrate you have understood each of the requirements and have addressed it in your solution. 
In this process, you may think of more requirements or things which the customer has not addressed.
A number of these requirements will overlap in your solution so structure your solution description to show how you are meeting all of the requirements. 

You should also think about the customer's deployment and operational problems. 
He is going to be modifying his infrastructure very rapidly while the keeps his existing systems running. 
Think about the manpower, skills and process for growing his infrastructure.

* How can he minimise the risks of wrong provisioning or falling behind.
* What is the lifecycle of deployment - racks, servers, power, software provisioning, testing.
* How many boxes will he provision each week.
* How might virtualisation or containerisation help.
* How much time and effort is saved through network automation - is it worth it?
* How will he monitor and test his infrastructure as it is deployed

### 3. Background Literature (Quality of references)     20%

You have been given some description of the technology in class however you need to show us you have read around the subject.
You need to find good references which back up your solution and show alternatives considered. 
Your references should follow the Harvard referencing format with citations in the text and a reference list at the end. 
Use the tools which the library provides to help you in this.


### 4. Proof of concept implementation  (based on class examples - appendix) - This will be demonstrated in class  20%

The class exercises cover examples of most of the technology you need to deliver the customer solution.
Your main text should describe how all of these technologies can work together to deliver a solution.

If you have written up each of the class exercises, you will have plenty of material to prove you understand the technology. 
Use code and log snippets, screenshots and your personal notes to describe each technology you have experimented with and the things you learnt.

Put your notes in the appendix and / or reference them in your github repo.

I want you to prove here that you understand the technology and have experimented with it in order to prove that it can be used by the customer.

The appendices are YOUR notes. They should record your work in such a way that you could come back to it and remember what you did. 
It evidences your work and understanding but it can be written in bullet points and rough notes. 

### 5. Conclusion and recommendations      20%

Having describe the customer's problem and your solution, your conclusion should summarise what you have done and make some recommendations.

You might want to consider:

* what have you managed to get to work and what didn't work in the time available
* what further investigation is needed
* what do you believe the customer also needs but you have not addressed
* what further information may be required from the customer.

Why do you think your solution is a good starting point and what are the next steps
